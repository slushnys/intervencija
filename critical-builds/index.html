Production-ready, defined

Done means it runs,
not just that it works.

Critical software is judged in production. Before go-live, the build is checked against a standard like this one, and the proposal states which items apply to your system.

  • Failure handling

    Timeouts, retries, idempotency and dead-letter handling for every external dependency.

  • Data integrity

    Clear transaction boundaries, reconciliation and audit trails wherever money or orders move.

  • Automated tests

    Tests at the levels that catch real regressions, running on every change.

  • Repeatable releases

    Automated builds and deployments, infrastructure as code, and a rehearsed rollback path.

  • Observability

    Structured logs, metrics and alerts tied to business outcomes, not just servers.

  • Security baseline

    Secrets management, least-privilege access and dependency scanning from the first release.

  • Capacity

    Load validated against realistic peaks, with known limits and known behaviour beyond them.

  • Operability

    Runbooks, ownership and escalation paths your team can rely on at three in the morning.

Before we begin

Useful details.
Clear expectations.

Can you build alongside our engineering team?

Yes. Responsibilities, code review, delivery tools and integration points can be agreed around your existing team and partners.

What if we only have a business idea?

We can begin by shaping the requirements, identifying the critical assumptions and defining a focused first scope before estimating the full build.

Can you work with legacy systems?

Yes. We start with the interfaces, dependencies and operational constraints that shape the change. The approach may be an integration, an extension or a phased replacement.

Who owns the code?

You do. Code, documentation and decision records are delivered into your repositories and tools, and the agreement assigns ownership of them to you.

How do you manage changes to scope?

Changes are discussed in terms of their effect on the outcome, dependencies, effort and delivery plan. The proposal sets out how changes are agreed, and nothing changes without your sign-off.

What happens after delivery?

Documentation, knowledge transfer and an agreed handover are part of planning the engagement. Any continuing support, maintenance or further development is scoped explicitly.
