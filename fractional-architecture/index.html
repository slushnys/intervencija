Working rhythm

What a month
can look like.

The cadence is agreed with you and adjusted as priorities change. A typical rhythm might include:

  1. A weekly architecture slot

    A standing session for design reviews and the questions blocking delivery.

  2. Asynchronous reviews

    Written feedback on proposals, RFCs and pull requests between sessions.

  3. A monthly roadmap review

    Technical priorities, risks and dependencies revisited with engineering leadership.

  4. Decision records kept current

    Significant decisions recorded as they are made, so the reasoning survives team changes.

Before we begin

Useful details.
Clear expectations.

How is this different from a system audit?

An audit is a focused review with a defined question and set of findings. Fractional Architecture provides continuing input across a sequence of decisions and delivery work.

Is this the same as a fractional CTO?

Not quite. A fractional CTO usually owns the whole technology function, including hiring, budgets and vendors. Fractional Architecture focuses on technical direction: design decisions, standards and architecture ownership, in support of the leaders you already have.

Will you replace our engineering leadership?

No. The role supports your existing leaders and teams. Decision authority and responsibilities are agreed explicitly so the engagement fits your organisation.

How much time can be allocated?

The cadence depends on your priorities, team structure and the decisions in scope. Time allocation, availability and review points are set out in the proposal.

Can this include hands-on engineering?

The architecture remit can include technical investigation and implementation guidance. A substantial build is scoped explicitly so delivery responsibilities remain clear.

How are the term and fees agreed?

The proposal defines the working rhythm, responsibilities, fees, engagement term, and how the work is reviewed or concluded.
