System Audits

Make the important decision with a clearer system view.

An independent senior review before a major platform, modernisation, data, or delivery commitment. The audit connects technical reality with operational importance and the decisions leadership needs to make.

When an audit fits

Before the organisation commits to a consequential path.

Modernisation

A legacy platform must evolve without losing the operation it already supports.

Platform decision

Build, buy, replace, or integrate decisions need a view beyond the immediate feature list.

Delivery reset

A programme has slowed, ownership is diffuse, or technical risk is difficult to prioritise.

Data or AI foundation

New capability depends on trustworthy data, permissions, governance, and production fit.

Audit scope

Focused on the decision, broad enough to expose its dependencies.

The exact scope follows the operating context. The audit avoids broad activity that does not change the decision.

Architecture

Domain boundaries, runtime topology, integration contracts, resilience, cloud posture, and change sensitivity.

Data

Sources, ownership, transformations, reporting paths, quality concerns, permissions, and operational use.

Delivery

Team boundaries, decision flow, release practices, observability, operational feedback, and accumulated risk.

Operating context

Revenue, service, compliance, reporting, fulfilment, and the people who carry the workflow every day.

Working sequence

Evidence first, recommendation second.

  1. 01 - Frame

    Define the decision.

    Align on the question, business dependency, stakeholders, and useful depth.

  2. 02 - Examine

    Review the system.

    Combine interviews, artefacts, code and architecture review, and workflow mapping as appropriate.

  3. 03 - Test

    Challenge the options.

    Make assumptions, trade-offs, dependencies, and operational consequences explicit.

  4. 04 - Decide

    Set the path.

    Deliver the decision record, recommended direction, and practical sequence.

Typical deliverables

Outputs designed to support a real decision.

  • A concise current-state system and workflow view
  • Risks, dependencies, assumptions, and ownership concerns
  • Architecture or delivery options with explicit trade-offs
  • A recommendation and sequenced next steps
  • Decision notes suitable for leadership and delivery alignment

Boundaries

An audit is not open-ended consultancy.

The engagement is anchored to a decision and a useful output. It does not replace formal regulatory certification, penetration testing, a financial audit, or specialist legal review. Where another discipline is required, that dependency is made explicit rather than implied.

Questions

Before an audit begins.

Can an audit work with incomplete documentation?

Yes. Existing artefacts are useful, but interviews, system evidence, and workflow mapping can establish a practical view. Documentation gaps may themselves be relevant findings.

Does the audit require access to source code?

Not always. Access depends on the decision. Architecture, integrations, operational behavior, data paths, and ownership may be more important than line-level review.

Can the audit lead into implementation?

Yes, where that is useful. The audit remains valuable as an independent decision record whether implementation stays internal, moves to another partner, or continues with Intervencija.
